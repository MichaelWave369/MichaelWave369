## What I build

I started with real-world network infrastructure: equipment, connections, deployment constraints, troubleshooting, and the fact that eventually a system has to work outside a diagram. Today I apply that field perspective to local computing, multi-model collaboration, research instruments, accessible creative software, and games.

**This is not a single giant product.** It is a growing family of original, independently developed tools and experiments that share a practical design principle:

> Make capability useful, keep evidence inspectable, and leave consequential decisions with people.

The public repositories include working prototypes, research protocols, software under active development, and speculative designs. **A passing build is not a production certification; an experimental result is not a universal scientific finding.** Each repository describes its own evidence and limitations.

## Research with receipts

**[Nested Bubble Gear / Altermath](https://github.com/MichaelWave369/NestedBubbleGear)** investigates when a coarse observation hides internal structure that still matters causally. The research program uses finite models, control experiments, explicit falsifiers, replayable artifacts, and a claim firewall. It does not assert a new physical law merely because a constructed experiment passes.

**[CollaborationJunction](https://github.com/MichaelWave369/CollaborationJunction)** examines human–AI collaboration and where AI is actually present in existing technology. Its evidence atlas separates sourced descriptions from independent tests; proposed human studies remain proposals until run.

**[FieldManual369](https://github.com/MichaelWave369/FieldManual369)** turns systems thinking, evidence, design, governance, and cybersecurity into an interactive learning environment.

**[Φ369 Research Lab on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/communities/enter-the-field-phi369/records)** houses versioned public research records and protocols. Negative results are part of the research, not something to hide.

## How I work

**Need or question → observable definition → smallest useful implementation → tests and counterexamples → evidence receipt → review → human decision → revise.**

- **Evidence over confidence.** Label what was observed, inferred, simulated, or left unknown.
- **Capability is not permission.** Tools, models, and agents operate within explicit authority boundaries.
- **Local-first where practical.** Prefer inspectable, user-owned capability over unnecessary dependencies.
- **Original implementations.** Study ideas; design and write our own systems rather than silently importing someone else’s work.
- **Design for real humans.** Accessibility, affordability, curiosity, play, and maintainability matter.

My toolbox spans network fieldwork, TypeScript/React/Next.js, Python, Rust, local inference with Ollama, GitHub Actions, visual tooling, audio, and game systems. The stack is less important than being able to explain what a system really did.
